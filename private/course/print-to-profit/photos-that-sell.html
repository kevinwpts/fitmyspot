Quick answer

You need a phone, a window and a plain background. Take 5 photos: the hero shot, in use, size, details and variations. Good photos are the cheapest way to sell more. In this chapter you'll set up a mini studio in 10 minutes.

Why photos matter so much

Online, buyers can't touch your product. The photo is the product. Two shops can sell the same thing at the same price, and the one with better photos gets almost all the sales.

The good news: you don't need a camera, a lightbox or editing skills.

Your 10-minute home studio

A window

Daylight from the side. No direct sun.

White paper or card

A large sheet as background. Curve it up the wall.

A table

Near the window.

Your phone

The main camera, not the selfie one.

White card as reflector

Bounces light into the shadows.

1

Set up by a window

Put a table next to a window, with the light coming from the side. Cloudy days are perfect. Direct sun gives hard shadows; if the sun shines in, hang a thin white sheet or baking paper over the window.

Turn off the ceiling lights. Mixed light gives strange colors.

2

Make a seamless background

Tape a large sheet of white or light grey card to the wall and let it curve down onto the table. No line where wall meets table = the product looks like it floats.

For a warmer look, try a wooden board or a linen cloth. Keep the same background for all your products so your shop looks consistent.

3

Fill the shadows

Hold a piece of white card on the side opposite the window. Watch the shadows get softer. That's all a reflector is.

4

Set up your phone

  • Wipe the lens (seriously, it makes a big difference)
  • Use the main camera, 1× or 2× zoom (not 0.5×)
  • Tap the product to focus, then slide brightness a little up
  • No flash, ever
  • Rest the phone on something, or use a cheap tripod, to avoid blur

The 5 photos every listing needs

5

1. The hero shot

The product alone, on your clean background, filling most of the frame. Slight angle from above. This is the photo people see in search, so it must be clear even when it's tiny.

6

2. In use

The product doing its job: the drawer full of tidy things, the cable tray under a real desk, the name sign on a kid's door. This is the photo that sells, because buyers picture it in their home.

7

3. Size

Show how big it is. A hand holding it, next to a common object, or with a ruler. "Smaller than I expected" is one of the most common complaints, and this photo prevents it.

8

4. Details

Close-up of the best part: the clean layer lines, the fit, the engraved name, the click. Shows quality.

9

5. Variations

All the colors or sizes together in one photo. Makes choosing easy, and often sells a second one.

Bonus: a 5–10 second video

A short clip of the product in use, or being printed, is great for listings that allow video and for Instagram. Film it vertically.

Quick editing (2 minutes)

Use your phone's built-in editor. Only these:

Setting What to do
Crop Square (1:1) or 4:5. Product centered.
Brightness A little up, so the white background is white
Straighten Lines straight
Colors Don't change. The color must match what arrives.

Honest photos only

Never use other people's photos, renders that look better than the real print, or filters that change the color. It leads to bad reviews and returns.

Common mistakes

Do

  • Daylight from the side
  • Plain, consistent background
  • Product fills the frame
  • Show it in use
  • Show the size

Don't

  • Flash or yellow ceiling light
  • Messy room in the background
  • Tiny product in a big empty photo
  • Only one photo
  • Color filters

Your task for this chapter

  • Set up your window studio (10 minutes)
  • Take the 5 photos of your product
  • Edit: crop, brightness, straighten
  • Pick your best hero shot and ask a friend: "What is this and would you buy it?"
