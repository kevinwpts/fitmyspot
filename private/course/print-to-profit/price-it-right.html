The short answer

Price = all your costs × 2 to 3, plus fees. Count filament, power, machine wear, your time and packaging. Then check your price against the market and adjust. You'll fill in a real worksheet for your product in this chapter.

Why most sellers charge too little

They look at the filament: "It's only $1 of plastic, I'll sell it for $6." Then they forget:

Their time

Removing supports, packing, messages. Often 15–30 min per order.

Failed prints

Every few prints, one fails. Someone pays for it. You.

Packaging

Box, padding, label.

Fees

Marketplaces and payments take 5–15%.

Selling cheap doesn't get you more sales. It gets you more work for nothing, and it makes buyers think your product is cheap.

05

Add it up and multiply

Total cost = material + power + machine + time + packaging

Price = total cost × 2.5 (use 2 for simple items, 3+ for personalized or unique ones)

Why multiply? Your profit pays for things you don't see per order: designs that didn't sell, test prints, photos, your printer upgrade, and your taxes.

06

Add the fees

Marketplaces take a cut of the price. To keep your profit, add the fees on top:

final price = (price + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %)

Example: price $20, fees 9.5% + $0.45 → ($20 + $0.45) ÷ 0.905 = $22.60

Check it against the market

Now compare with similar listings:

Your price vs. the market What to do
About the same 👍 Good. Win on photos and fit.
Higher Fine if yours is better or more specific. Show why in photos.
Much higher Lower your costs (faster print, less support, less packing time)
Much lower Raise your price. You're leaving money on the table.

Never compete on being cheapest

Someone will always be cheaper. Compete on fit, quality, photos and personalization instead.

4 ways to earn more per sale

Sell sets

A set of 4 costs you almost the same time as 1.

Personalize

A name or color choice can add 30–100% to the price.

Speed up printing

Fewer supports, thicker layers where it doesn't show.

Offer a premium version

A "pro" color or material at a higher price.

How to handle shipping in your price

Two simple options:

  • Free shipping, built into the price. Buyers love it. Add your average shipping cost to the price.
  • Shipping charged separately. Fine for local or heavy items.

Whichever you choose, check real postage rates for your country before you publish the price. More in chapter 8.

Insider pricing moves

01

Move shipping into the price (US buyers)

Etsy prioritizes US listings with shipping under $6 in search. If your real shipping is $8, set shipping to $5.95 and add the difference to the item price. Same total for the buyer, better ranking for you. Etsy even has a shipping price tool that does this for US listings.

02

Make a "good, better, best"

Offer three versions in one listing with variations:

Option Example Price
Single 1 cable clip $6
Set of 4 (most pick this) 4 clips $15
Set of 10 10 clips $29

Most buyers pick the middle option. The single makes the set look like a deal. The big set makes the middle look reasonable.

03

Charge for personalization separately

Add personalization as a variation ("+ name: $4") instead of including it for free. Buyers who want it happily pay, and you get paid for the extra minutes it takes.

04

Check your real hourly rate monthly

(revenue − all costs) ÷ hours worked

If it's below what you'd accept for a normal side job, raise prices or drop the product that takes the most time per dollar.
