faq:
  - q: Do I need a lightbox to photograph 3D prints?
    a: No. Soft daylight from a window and a sheet of white card work just as well, and often look more natural.
  - q: What background is best for 3D print photos?
    a: A plain white or light grey background for the main photo, so the product stands out in search. Use real settings (a desk, a shelf) for the in-use photos.

::: answer
**Window light from the side + white card background + your phone's main camera.** Take 5 photos: hero, in use, size, details and colors. No flash, no filters.
:::

## Set up in 10 minutes

::: step home | Find a window
Table next to a window, light from the side. Cloudy days are perfect. Turn off ceiling lights.
:::

::: step file | Make a background
Tape white card to the wall and curve it onto the table. No corner line = clean look.
:::

::: step palette | Soften shadows
Hold white card opposite the window. It bounces light into the shadows.
:::

::: step camera | Set your phone
Clean the lens. Main camera, 1× or 2×. Tap to focus, nudge brightness up. Rest the phone on something steady.
:::

## The 5 photos that sell

::: icons
star | 1. Hero | Product alone, clean background, fills the frame.
hand | 2. In use | Doing its job in a real home. This one sells.
ruler | 3. Size | In a hand or next to a ruler.
search | 4. Details | Close-up of the best part.
palette | 5. Variations | All colors or sizes together.
:::

## Do and don't

::: yesno | Do | Don't
+ Soft daylight from the side
+ The same background on every product
+ Product fills most of the photo
+ Show the size
- Flash or yellow lamps
- A messy room behind it
- Color filters
- Renders that look better than the real thing
:::

::: tip | Quick edit
Crop square or 4:5, a little brightness, straighten. Don't change the colors: the buyer should get what they saw.
:::

::: do | Do this today
- Set up the window studio
- Take the 5 photos of one product
- Swap your current main photo for the new hero shot
:::
