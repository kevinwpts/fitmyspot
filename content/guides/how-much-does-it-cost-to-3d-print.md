faq:
  - q: How much does a 3D print cost in filament?
    a: Most PLA costs about $15–25 per kg, so 2–3 cents per gram. A small keychain (10 g) costs about 20–30 cents in filament, a 100 g organizer about $2–3.
  - q: How much electricity does a 3D printer use?
    a: A typical home FDM printer uses roughly 100–150 W while printing, so a 5-hour print uses about 0.5–0.75 kWh. At $0.25 per kWh that's around 13–19 cents.
  - q: Is 3D printing cheaper than buying?
    a: For small plastic parts, often yes, in material cost. But if you count your time, failed prints and the printer itself, buying is sometimes cheaper. Printing wins when the part is custom or hard to find.

::: answer
**Material + power is usually under $1–3 per print.** The real costs are machine wear, failed prints and **your time**. For selling, count it all. Use the free [price calculator](/tools/3d-print-price-calculator) to get your exact number.
:::

## The 5 costs of a 3D print

::: icons
printer | Filament | $15–25 per kg for most PLA. 2–3 cents per gram.
plug | Electricity | About 100–150 W while printing.
wrench | Machine wear | Nozzles, plates, belts, and one day a new printer.
x | Failed prints | Add about 10% for the ones that go wrong.
clock | Your time | Prep, cleanup, supports. Usually the biggest cost.
:::

## Example costs

Prices: $20 per kg filament, 120 W printer, $0.25 per kWh, $0.25 per hour machine wear.

| Print | Filament | Time | Material + power + wear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keychain | 10 g | 30 min | **~$0.35** |
| Phone stand | 40 g | 2 h | **~$1.45** |
| Drawer organizer | 120 g | 6 h | **~$4.30** |
| Large planter | 400 g | 18 h | **~$14** |

Add your time on top. 15 minutes of hands-on work at $20/hour is **$5**, more than the materials for most prints.

## Work out your own cost

::: step printer | Filament
`spool price ÷ spool grams × grams used`

Your slicer shows the grams. Add 10% for waste and failed prints.
:::

::: step plug | Electricity
`watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × price per kWh`

Check your electricity bill for the price per kWh.
:::

::: step wrench | Machine wear
`printer price ÷ expected hours + parts`

A $600 printer over 3,000 hours is $0.20 per hour. Round up for parts.
:::

::: step clock | Your time (if you sell)
`hands-on minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate`

Only for prints you sell. For your own use, skip it.
:::

## How to make prints cheaper

::: icons
zap | Less infill | 10–15% infill is enough for most decorative and light-use parts.
list | Fewer supports | Rotate or split the model so it needs less support.
box | Fill the plate | Print several at once. Same prep time, more parts.
coins | Buy filament in bulk | Multi-spool packs are often cheaper per kg.
:::
