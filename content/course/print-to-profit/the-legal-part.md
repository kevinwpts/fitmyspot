::: answer
Five checks before you sell anything: **1)** you have the right to sell the design, **2)** it follows the marketplace's rules, **3)** no logos or characters, **4)** it's safe for its use, **5)** you know your basic tax and business rules. Ten minutes per product saves your shop.
:::

::: note | Not legal or tax advice
This is a plain-English overview to keep you out of the most common trouble. Rules differ per country and change over time. When in doubt, check the official source for your country.
:::

## The 5-check list

::: figure license
:::


::: step scale | Check 1: Do you have the right to sell it?
| Where the design comes from | Can you sell prints? |
|---|---|
| **You designed it** | ✅ Yes |
| CC0 / CC BY / CC BY-SA / CC BY-ND | ✅ Yes, with credit |
| Anything with **NC** | ❌ No |
| "Personal use" / MakerWorld Standard license | ❌ No, without permission |
| A **commercial license** you bought | ✅ Within its terms |

Save a screenshot of the license with the date. Licenses can change.

**Fonts count too.** Text on a product (a name sign, a keychain) uses a font, and fonts have licenses. Fonts from **Google Fonts** use the Open Font License, which lets you print them on products you sell. Many "free" fonts on download sites are **personal use only**. Check before you use one, and keep a list of the fonts you use.

Full guide: [Can you sell 3D prints?](/guides/can-you-sell-3d-prints)
:::

::: step store | Check 2: Does the marketplace allow it?
Marketplaces add their own rules **on top of** licenses.

- **Etsy:** 3D printed items must be **your own design**, or personalized for the buyer (since 2025). A commercial license isn't enough.
- **Other marketplaces:** read their rules for handmade or made-to-order items before listing.
- **Your own shop and local sales:** only the license and the law apply.

More: [Selling 3D prints on Etsy](/guides/selling-3d-prints-on-etsy)
:::

::: step x | Check 3: No logos, characters or brand names as decoration
Never use other people's:

- Characters (games, films, cartoons)
- Logos and brand names on the product
- Designs that copy a famous product's look

**What's usually fine:** saying what it's **compatible** with, in plain words: "Fits IKEA Alex drawers" or "Holder for Nintendo Switch games". Don't use their logo, and don't make it look official.
:::

::: step shield | Check 4: Is it safe for what it's used for?
Ask: **what happens if this breaks or is used wrong?**

::: yesno | Low risk, good to start | High risk, avoid for now
+ Organizers, holders, stands
+ Decorations and signs
+ Replacement parts for non-safety things (shelf pegs, knobs)
+ Personalized keychains and tags (for adults)
- Toys for young children (choking parts, strict toy rules)
- Anything for food or drink, unless you know food-safe printing
- Safety parts: helmets, brakes, child seats, gas, electrical
- Anything that holds weight overhead
:::

**Selling in the EU?** The EU's product safety rules (GPSR) apply to products sold to EU consumers, including from small sellers. In practice: put **your name and contact details** and a **product identifier** (like a batch or product number) on the product, its packaging or a document with it, and include safety info where it's needed. Marketplaces like Etsy ask for this information too.
:::

::: step file | Check 5: Taxes and business basics
When you start selling regularly, it usually becomes a business in the eyes of the tax office. In most countries that means:

1. **Register** as a sole trader or small business (often free and online)
2. **Keep every receipt:** filament, parts, packaging, fees
3. **Track every sale** in a simple spreadsheet
4. **Check VAT / sales tax** rules: many countries have a threshold before you need to register for VAT
5. **Declare the income** in your yearly tax return

::: tip | Set aside 30%
Until you know your exact numbers, put about 30% of every payout in a separate account for taxes. Better to get money back than to owe it.
:::
:::

## What Etsy's rule actually says

In June 2025 Etsy changed one sentence that matters a lot for 3D printing. Items "made by a seller" (which includes 3D printing) **"must be produced based on a seller's original design"**. The old text also allowed "a templated design or pattern". That word is gone.

What it means in practice:

- A free or paid model from someone else = not your original design, even if the license allows selling.
- Changing the text on someone else's name-sign template = still their template.
- Your own design, even a very simple one, = fine.

::: step file | Keep a design log
For every product, keep a folder with: your CAD file, 2–3 screenshots of the design process with dates, and the first test print photo. If Etsy ever questions a listing, you can prove it's yours in 5 minutes.
:::

::: step store | Fill in the "who made it" questions honestly
When you create a listing, Etsy asks who made it and when. Answer **"I did"** and **"Made to order"** only if that's true. If someone else prints for you (a production partner), Etsy requires you to disclose that partner in your shop.
:::

::: step shield | Add the EU safety info in Etsy
If you sell to buyers in the EU, Etsy asks for product safety information (GPSR). Fill in your name, address and contact as the responsible person, add a product identifier (for example a product code like `BP-CAB-01`) and any warnings. Put the same info on a small card in the package.

Where: in each listing, scroll to **Product safety** (or set it once for the shop under **Settings → Options**). Etsy moves this now and then, so search for "GPSR" in Etsy's help if you can't find it.
:::

::: do | Before you list a product
- I designed it, or I have a license that allows selling (screenshot saved)
- It follows the marketplace's rules
- No characters, logos or brand names as decoration
- It's low-risk for its use (not a toy for kids, not food, not safety)
- EU buyers: my name, contact and a product ID are on the product, packaging or included note
- The sale goes into my sales spreadsheet
:::

## What if someone copies your design?

It happens, and it's annoying. What you can do:

::: icons
camera | Save proof | Keep your CAD files, dates and first photos.
msg | Report it | Marketplaces have forms for reporting copied designs.
star | Stay ahead | Improve your product, add personalization, build your name. Copycats rarely keep up.
:::
