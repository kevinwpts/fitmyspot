::: answer
Design your product to **ship flat and small**, buy packaging **in bulk**, check real postage **before** you set prices, and always ship with **tracking**. A good unboxing (a thank-you note and a clean pack) gets you reviews and repeat buyers.
:::

## Shipping can eat your whole profit

A $20 product that costs $8 to ship and $1.50 to pack only works if you planned for it. Most beginners find out after the first order. You'll plan it now.

## Plan shipping in 5 steps

::: figure packing
:::


::: step ruler | Design for the mailbox
The cheapest shipping is usually for **flat, light parcels** (often called large letter or small packet). Check your national post's size limits and design around them:

- Can it print in **parts** that fit together?
- Can it be **thinner** without getting weak?
- Can it **nest** or stack?

A few millimetres can halve your shipping cost.
:::

::: step search | Check real postage prices
Weigh your product **with packaging** and measure the box. Then check prices at 2–3 carriers for:

- Your own country
- Your nearest neighbour countries
- The US / your biggest market

Write them in a small table. Use these numbers in your price (chapter 6).
:::

::: step box | Choose your packaging
| Product | Packaging |
|---|---|
| Flat, sturdy items | Padded envelope or stiff mailer |
| Small fragile items | Small box + paper padding |
| Larger items | Box with 2–3 cm padding on all sides |

Buy in bulk (50–100). The price per piece drops a lot. Recycled paper padding looks better and is better for the planet than bubble wrap.
:::

::: step check | Make a packing station
One shelf or box with everything: mailers, padding, tape, labels, thank-you cards, a scale. Packing an order should take **3–5 minutes**, not 15. That's time you charge for.
:::

::: step truck | Always use tracking
Tracking protects you when a buyer says "it never arrived". On most marketplaces, an untracked lost parcel is your loss. For cheap items, a tracked letter option is often enough.
:::

## The unboxing that brings reviews

Put your shop name and Instagram on it. Happy buyers come back and tag you.

## When something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Arrived broken | Ask for a photo, send a new one. Improve your packing. |
| Lost in the post | Check tracking, then reprint or refund. Stay friendly. |
| "Smaller than expected" | Refund or replace kindly, then add a size photo to the listing |
| Wrong item sent | Send the right one first, sort out the return after |

A friendly fix often turns an angry buyer into a 5-star review.

::: warn | Shipping abroad
Outside your trade area (for example from the EU to the UK or US), parcels may need a customs form, and the buyer may pay import fees. Say this clearly in your listing.
:::

## Money-saving shipping moves

::: step tag | Buy labels through the marketplace
In many countries (including the US, UK and Canada), Etsy sells discounted shipping labels right in the order screen. It's often cheaper than the post office counter, adds tracking automatically and marks the order as shipped.

How: **Shop Manager → Orders & Shipping →** open the order **→ Purchase shipping label →** check the weight and size **→** pick the cheapest tracked option **→ Buy → Print.** A cheap thermal label printer saves a lot of time once you ship a few orders a week.
:::

::: step ruler | Design to the size limit, not the product
Find your post's cheapest parcel size (often around 2–3 cm thick for large letters). Design flat items, like signs and trays, to fit **just under** it. Print taller items in parts that click together.
:::

::: step truck | US buyers: keep shipping under $6
Etsy prioritizes US listings with shipping under $6. Move the rest into the item price (chapter 6).
:::

::: step box | Make a 3-minute packing kit
Pre-fold boxes, pre-cut padding, pre-written thank-you cards. Pack 5 orders at once at a fixed time each day, not one by one.
:::
