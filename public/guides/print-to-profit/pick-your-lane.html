Start here, pick your lane

What your printer can realistically earn, the 7 ways to make money with it, and a quick quiz to pick the one that fits you.

In this chapter · 5 parts
  1. Quick quiz: which lane fits you?
  2. The 7 lanes
  3. Why one lane beats five

Your printer can pay for itself, and then some. Not with luck. With a plan.

Here's the honest math. The example product in this guide sells for $17 and leaves about $12 after filament, power, packaging and fees:

  • 10 sales a month: about $120
  • 30 sales a month: about $370
  • 100 sales a month (with batch printing): about $1,230

That's before your time, and it's not a promise. Plenty of sellers never get past their first few sales. The ones who do have one thing in common: they picked one thing and did it properly. That's what this chapter is about.

In 30 days you'll have: your own product, a price that actually pays, a live listing and your first buyers. Let's start.

The short answer

In this chapter you'll pick one way to make money with your printer, set a realistic first goal, and block time in your week for it. That's it. One decision, made properly.

Quick quiz: which lane fits you?

There are 7 ways to make money with a printer. Answer 4 questions and see which one fits you. It takes 30 seconds.

1. How fast do you want your first sale?
2. How do you feel about 3D design (CAD)?
3. How many hours a week can you spend?
4. What sounds most fun?

Not sure?

If you're torn, pick Personalized items or Your own designs. They work on every marketplace, including Etsy, and they're where most sellers end up anyway.

The 7 lanes

Print for people nearby

Friends, neighbours, local groups. Custom bits and fixes. No shop, no shipping.

Personalized items

Names, pets, dates. Buyers pay more for "made for me". Allowed on Etsy.

Your own designs

Products you design and sell anywhere. Where the real money is.

Replacement parts

The broken dishwasher wheel. People search for exact parts and pay well.

Sell the files

Design once, sell many times. Slow to start, needs a following.

Print for businesses

Signs, displays, jigs, prototypes. Bigger orders that repeat.

Make content

Videos of what you make. An audience that later buys from you.

Why one lane beats five

People who try a bit of everything (a keychain here, a random model there) rarely get traction. People who pick one lane become "the shop with the great board game inserts", and buyers remember them.

Kevin's tip

You can change lanes later

Picking one doesn't lock you in. It's for the next 30 days. After your first sales, adding a second lane is easy.

Now make it real

01

Write down your lane

One sentence. Make it specific:

  • ❌ "I'll sell 3D prints."
  • ✅ "I'll sell personalized name signs for kids' rooms."
  • ✅ "I'll make organizers for board game boxes."

Notice the pattern: what + for whom.

02

Set a realistic first goal

Your first goal is not income. It's proof.

Goal When
First product ready and photographed Day 10
First sale Day 20
10 sales Day 30–60

When you hit 10 sales, you know people want it. Then you grow it.

03

Block time in your week

Put it in your calendar like an appointment. For example:

  • Tuesday evening: design and print (2 hours)
  • Saturday morning: photos, listing, posts (2 hours)

Four focused hours a week beats a whole weekend once a month.

04

Know what you won't do

Saying no is part of the plan. For the next 30 days:

  • No second lane
  • No buying a new printer
  • No selling other people's designs
  • No pricing by filament cost

The trap: endless research

It's tempting to keep reading and planning. Don't. The next chapters each end with a task. Do the task, then move on.
