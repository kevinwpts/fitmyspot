How Much Does a 3D Print Really Cost? (Hint: Not the Filament)

From a keychain to a big organizer: what a print really costs you, and the cost almost everyone forgets.

FreeBy KevinUpdated November 20, 20264 min read

The short answer

Material + power is usually under $1–3 per print. The real costs are machine wear, failed prints and your time. For selling, count it all. Use the free price calculator to get your exact number.

The 5 costs of a 3D print

Filament

$15–25 per kg for most PLA. 2–3 cents per gram.

Electricity

About 100–150 W while printing.

Machine wear

Nozzles, plates, belts, and one day a new printer.

Failed prints

Add about 10% for the ones that go wrong.

Your time

Prep, cleanup, supports. Usually the biggest cost.

Example costs

Prices: $20 per kg filament, 120 W printer, $0.25 per kWh, $0.25 per hour machine wear.

Print Filament Time Material + power + wear
Keychain 10 g 30 min ~$0.35
Phone stand 40 g 2 h ~$1.45
Drawer organizer 120 g 6 h ~$4.30
Large planter 400 g 18 h ~$14

Add your time on top. 15 minutes of hands-on work at $20/hour is $5, more than the materials for most prints.

Work out your own cost

01

Filament

spool price ÷ spool grams × grams used

Your slicer shows the grams. Add 10% for waste and failed prints.

02

Electricity

watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × price per kWh

Check your electricity bill for the price per kWh.

03

Machine wear

printer price ÷ expected hours + parts

A $600 printer over 3,000 hours is $0.20 per hour. Round up for parts.

04

Your time (if you sell)

hands-on minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate

Only for prints you sell. For your own use, skip it.

How to make prints cheaper

Less infill

10–15% infill is enough for most decorative and light-use parts.

Fewer supports

Rotate or split the model so it needs less support.

Fill the plate

Print several at once. Same prep time, more parts.

Buy filament in bulk

Multi-spool packs are often cheaper per kg.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 3D print cost in filament?

Most PLA costs about $15–25 per kg, so 2–3 cents per gram. A small keychain (10 g) costs about 20–30 cents in filament, a 100 g organizer about $2–3.

How much electricity does a 3D printer use?

A typical home FDM printer uses roughly 100–150 W while printing, so a 5-hour print uses about 0.5–0.75 kWh. At $0.25 per kWh that's around 13–19 cents.

Is 3D printing cheaper than buying?

For small plastic parts, often yes, in material cost. But if you count your time, failed prints and the printer itself, buying is sometimes cheaper. Printing wins when the part is custom or hard to find.
