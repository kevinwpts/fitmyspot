How to ship 3D prints

Pack fast, ship cheap, and make sure your prints arrive in one piece.

FreeBy KevinUpdated November 20, 20264 min read

Quick answer

Small and flat ships cheapest. Check real postage before you set a price, fill every gap so nothing moves, and always use tracking. Planning shipping first can save more than any filament deal.

Ship it in 6 steps

1

Design for the mailbox

Check your post's size limits for flat parcels or large letters. A few millimetres thinner can halve the price. Can it print in parts, or lie flat?

2

Check real prices

Weigh the product with packaging. Compare 2–3 carriers for your country, your neighbours and your biggest market. Write the prices down.

3

Pick the right packaging

Product Use
Flat and sturdy Padded envelope
Small and fragile Small box + paper padding
Large Box with 2–3 cm padding all round
4

Do the shake test

Close the package and shake it. If you hear or feel movement, add padding. Protect thin parts like spikes, handles and text.

5

Use tracking

Tracking protects you when a parcel "never arrived". For cheap items, a tracked letter is often enough.

6

Add a thank-you note

One handwritten line and your shop name. It brings reviews and repeat buyers.

Cheap packaging that works

Recycled paper padding

Cheap, light, looks good.

Mailers in bulk

50–100 at a time drops the price per piece.

Thermal labels

Faster than printing on paper.

One look

Same tape, note and wrap every time.

Shipping abroad

Outside your trade area (for example EU to UK or US), parcels may need a customs form, and buyers may pay import fees. Say it in your listing.

Do this today

  • Weigh your product with packaging
  • Check postage at 2 carriers
  • Put shipping into your price with the calculator

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to ship 3D prints?

Design products to fit the post's flat parcel or large letter size, use light padded mailers, and buy packaging in bulk. Size and weight decide most of the price.

How do I stop 3D prints breaking in the post?

Fill all empty space so nothing moves, protect thin parts with extra padding, and use a box for fragile items. Shake test it before you seal it.
