How to Make Money With a 3D Printer: 7 Ways That Actually Work

The 7 ways people actually earn with a home printer, what each pays, and which one to start with.

By KevinUpdated November 19, 20266 min read

The short answer

Pick one way to earn (below), make something for one specific group of people, check you're allowed to sell it, and price in your time. Most people make a side income, not a salary. That's fine: it's how everyone starts.

The 7 ways to earn

Print for people nearby

Friends, neighbours, local groups. Fastest first sale.

Sell your own designs

Where the real money is. You can sell them anywhere.

Personalized items

Names, pets, gifts. People pay more for "made for me".

Replacement parts

Broken clips, wheels, knobs. People search for exact parts.

Sell the files

Design once, sell many times on MakerWorld or Printables.

Print for businesses

Signs, displays, jigs. Bigger orders that repeat.

Make content

Videos of your prints can grow an audience that buys.

Kevin's tip

Pick just one

Trying all seven at once is the #1 reason people give up. Choose one, get your first sale, then add more.

Now follow these steps.

01

Choose your way to earn

Use this quick guide:

  • Want a sale this week? Print for people nearby.
  • Want income that grows? Sell your own designs.
  • Easy start with designs? Personalized items.
  • Love CAD? Sell files and your own products.
  • Reliable printer? Print for businesses.
02

Pick one group of buyers

Not "everyone". Pick a group with a clear problem:

  • Board gamers with messy boxes
  • Cat owners
  • People with small flats
  • Car owners with broken clips

A specific buyer makes everything easier: what to make, where to post it, what to write.

03

Check you're allowed to sell it

This step saves your shop.

Safe to sell

  • Your own design
  • Models with a license that allows selling (like CC BY)
  • Personalized items made for the buyer

Not safe

  • Models marked NC, "personal use" or MakerWorld's Standard license
  • Characters, logos, brand names
  • Other people's designs on Etsy (not allowed since 2025)

→ Licenses explained simply

04

Price it so it's worth your time

Filament is the smallest cost. Count it all:

  1. Filament
  2. Electricity
  3. Machine wear
  4. Your time (usually the biggest cost)
  5. Packaging
  6. Fees

Then multiply by 2 to 3 for profit.

→ Use the free price calculator

05

Take good photos

Phone + window light is enough.

  • Clean, plain background
  • One photo in use
  • One showing size (a hand or a ruler)
  • Daylight, no flash
06

Post it where your buyers are

  • Nearby: local Facebook groups, marketplaces
  • Online: Etsy (own designs only), your own shop
  • Your people: a hobby forum or group for your buyers

Write what it does, not what it is: "Keeps 8 Switch games tidy" beats "3D printed holder".

How much can you make?

Simple math: profit per item × sales per week × 4.

Profit per item Sales per week Per month
$5 5 $100
$8 10 $320
$12 20 $960

Be realistic

Many hobby sellers make under $200 a month. The ones who earn more sell their own designs, have great photos, and show up every week. Be careful with anyone promising "$5,000 a month".

What it costs to start

If you already own a printer, very little:

Filament

Most PLA costs about $15–25 per kg.

Electricity

A home printer uses about 100–150 W.

Wear parts

Nozzles, plates and belts wear out.

Packaging

Often the biggest cost per order.

Fees

Marketplaces like Etsy take a cut.

Good to know: what selling files pays

On MakerWorld, regular points buy gift cards (mostly for the Bambu Lab store). Only models in the Exclusive program earn points you can cash out, about $0.066 per point from $100. Models without a print profile or real photos earn far fewer points. Files are great passive income later, but rarely your first income.

Do this today

  • Write down 3 product ideas for one type of buyer
  • Check the license of anything you didn't design
  • Run your numbers in the price calculator
  • Post one item, in one place

Frequently asked questions

Can you really make money with a 3D printer?

Yes, but most people make a side income, not a salary. A realistic first goal is a few small sales a week. The people who earn the most sell their own designs or solve a specific problem for a specific group of buyers.

How much can a 3D printer make per month?

It depends far more on what you sell than on the printer. Many hobby sellers make under $200 a month. Sellers with their own designs, good photos and steady marketing can earn much more. Treat any "$5,000 a month" claim with suspicion.

Is it legal to sell 3D prints of models I downloaded?

Only if the license allows it. Many free models are for personal use only. Read our guide to 3D print licenses before you sell anything you didn't design yourself.

What is the easiest way to start?

Print-on-demand for people around you (custom parts, replacements, name tags) needs no shop and no design skills. It is the fastest way to your first sale.
