How to Price 3D Prints (Most Sellers Charge Half What They Should)

The simple formula that makes every sale worth your time, and why filament barely matters.

By KevinUpdated November 19, 20265 min read

The short answer

Add up all your costs, then multiply by 2 to 3. Filament is usually the smallest cost. Your time is usually the biggest.

→ Skip the math: use the free price calculator.

The formula

Price = (Filament + Power + Machine + Your time + Packaging) × 2.5 + Fees

Let's fill it in, one step at a time.

01

Filament

spool price ÷ spool grams × grams used

Your slicer shows the grams. Add 10% for failed prints and purges.

Example: $20 spool of 1,000 g = $0.02 per gram. A 60 g print = $1.20, plus 10% = $1.32.

02

Electricity

watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × price per kWh

A home printer uses about 100–150 W.

Example: 0.12 kW × 3 hours × $0.25 = $0.09. Small, but count it.

03

Machine wear

Nozzles, plates and the printer itself wear out. Pick a cost per print hour.

Example: a $600 printer that lasts 3,000 hours = $0.20/hour. Round up to $0.25 for parts. 3 hours = $0.75.

04

Your time

Only count hands-on time: removing supports, cleaning up, packing, messages.

Example: 15 minutes at $20/hour = $5.00. Bigger than everything above combined.

05

Packaging

Box, padding, label. Check real shipping prices in your country before you set a price.

Example: $1.00

06

Markup and fees

Your costs only get you to zero. Multiply by 2 to 3 for profit. Then add the marketplace fees on top, so they don't eat your profit.

The example, added up

Cost Amount
Filament $1.32
Electricity $0.09
Machine $0.75
Your time $5.00
Packaging $1.00
Total cost $8.16
× 2.5 markup $20.40

Round it to $20–21, then add fees. Note how small the filament is.

The #1 pricing mistake

Pricing by filament. A $1 print sold for $5 feels like profit. After your time, packing and fees, you worked for free.

3 tricks to earn more per sale

Sell sets

4 cable clips sell better than 1, and take almost the same time.

Offer personalization

A name on it can double what people will pay.

Don't race to the bottom

Someone selling for $3 isn't counting their time. They won't last.

Two moves that raise every order

01

Good, better, best

Offer a single, a set of 4 and a set of 10 in one listing. Most buyers pick the middle. The single makes the set look like a deal.

02

Keep US shipping under $6

Etsy prioritizes US listings with shipping under $6. Put the rest of the shipping cost into the item price. Same total, better ranking.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for a 3D print?

Add up material, electricity, machine wear, your hands-on time, packaging and selling fees, then multiply by 2 to 3 for profit. For most small items the real cost is far higher than the filament alone.

How do I calculate filament cost?

Divide the price of the spool by its weight in grams, then multiply by the grams your slicer reports for the print. A $20 spool of 1,000 g costs 2 cents per gram, so a 50 g print uses $1 of filament.

Should I charge per hour of printing?

Charge a small rate per print hour for machine wear and electricity, and a separate, higher rate for your own hands-on time. Your time is usually the bigger cost.
