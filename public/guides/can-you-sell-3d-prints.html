Can You Sell 3D Prints? The 30-Second Check That Saves Your Shop

One quick check tells you if you can legally sell a print. Skip it and your shop can get shut down.

By KevinUpdated November 19, 20265 min read

The short answer

Your own designs: yes, sell them anywhere. Other people's models: only if the license says so. "Free to download" does not mean "free to sell".

Not legal advice

This is a plain-English overview. Every model can have its own terms, and they can change. Always read the license on the model's page.

Check any model in 5 steps

01

Find the license

Open the model's page. The license is usually under the description or next to the download button.

02

Look for "NC" or "personal use"

If you see any of these, don't sell prints without written permission:

  • NC (non-commercial), like CC BY-NC
  • Personal use only
  • MakerWorld's Standard Digital File License
03

Look for a license that allows selling

These generally allow selling prints:

  • CC0 / public domain
  • CC BY (credit the designer)
  • CC BY-SA (credit + share remixes the same way)
  • CC BY-ND (credit + don't change the design)
04

Check for characters and brands

A character, logo or brand name? Don't sell it. Whatever license the uploader picked, the character still belongs to its owner.

05

Save proof

Screenshot the license and keep it with your notes. Licenses can change later, and the screenshot shows what it said when you started.

Licenses at a glance

You can sell prints

  • CC0 / public domain
  • CC BY, with credit
  • CC BY-SA, with credit
  • CC BY-ND, with credit, unchanged
  • A commercial license you bought, within its terms

You can't sell prints

  • Anything with NC
  • Personal use only
  • MakerWorld Standard Digital File License (without permission)
  • Characters, logos and brands

MakerWorld

Most MakerWorld models use the Standard Digital File License. It says you may not use the files or prints to make money without the creator's permission.

Two legal ways to sell:

A CC license that allows selling

Some models use one. Check the badge on the page.

Commercial license membership

Some creators sell a monthly membership that lets you sell their prints. Prices and terms vary per creator.

Printables and Thingiverse

The uploader picks a license per model, often Creative Commons. Use the table above. Buying a paid model doesn't automatically let you sell prints of it. Check that model's terms.

Etsy is stricter than any license

Since June 2025, Etsy only allows 3D prints of your own designs (or items personalized for the buyer). Even with a commercial license, someone else's model can get your listing removed. Read the Etsy guide.

The easy way out: design your own

Your own design = no license worries, sell anywhere, nobody can claim it. Most bestsellers are simple shapes made in free tools like Tinkercad or Onshape.

Keep a design log

For everything you sell, keep a folder with your CAD file, 2–3 dated screenshots of the design process and the first test print photo. If a marketplace or another designer questions your product, you can prove it's yours in minutes.

Do this today

  • Open the last model you printed and find its license
  • Is it NC or "personal use"? Then it's for you, not for sale
  • Write down one simple product you could design yourself instead

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell prints of free models from MakerWorld?

Usually not. Models under MakerWorld's Standard Digital File License may not be used to make money without the creator's permission. Some creators sell a commercial license membership, and some models use a Creative Commons license that allows selling.

What does CC BY-NC mean for 3D prints?

NC means non-commercial. You may print it for yourself, but you may not sell the prints. Any license with NC in it means no selling.

Can I sell prints if I credit the designer?

Only if the license allows commercial use. Credit alone is not permission. CC BY and CC BY-SA allow selling with credit; anything with NC does not.

Is it safe to sell prints of characters from films or games?

No. Characters, logos and brand names belong to their owners, whatever license the uploader put on the file. Selling them risks takedowns and legal claims.
