How to use these numbers

How the calculator works

It adds up every cost of one item: filament (with a buffer for failed prints), electricity, machine wear, your hands-on time and packaging. Then it applies your markup and adds marketplace fees on top, so the profit you see is what you actually keep.

Not sure about the numbers? Read how to price 3D prints for a full walkthrough.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the cost of a 3D print?

Add the filament cost (spool price ÷ grams × grams used), electricity (watts × hours × price per kWh), machine wear per hour, your hands-on time, packaging and selling fees.

What markup should I use for 3D prints?

Most sellers multiply their total cost by 2 to 3. Personalized or unique designs can go higher.

How much electricity does a 3D printer use?

A typical home FDM printer averages roughly 100–150 W while printing. A heated chamber or a large bed can use more.
